| | |
|---|---|
| **Vendor** | Vanta, Inc. (vanta.com) |

| **Requesting team** | Engineering / compliance (SOC 2 program tooling) |
| **Assessor** | Daniel Chavez |
| **Assessment date** | 2 September 2026 |
| **Method** | Public-source review only: vendor security page, information security addendum, DPA, MSA, SLA, help-center integration docs, press and third-party incident reporting. ## 1. Scope and inherent risk

**What the vendor does for us.** Connects read-only to our AWS accounts, identity provider, HR system and GitHub; continuously tests our controls against SOC 2 and ISO 27001; stores our policies, risk register, vendor inventory and audit evidence; hosts our public trust page; gives the auditor a portal into our evidence.

**Data the vendor touches.**

| Data class | Examples | Present? |
|---|---|---|
| Restricted | Card data, health data | No |
| Confidential | Full inventory of our cloud configuration and security findings; employee names, roles, MFA status, device compliance; access lists; audit evidence; vendor list; risk register | Yes, and unusually complete |
| Internal | Policies, control ownership, task assignments | Yes |
| Public | Trust page content | Yes |

The distinguishing feature of a compliance-automation vendor is that its dataset is a map of our weaknesses. A breach at Vanta exposes not customer PII but the list of which of our controls are failing and who has privileged access, which is reconnaissance material for an attacker.

**Inherent risk tiering.**

| Factor | Rating | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Data sensitivity | High | Aggregated security posture and personnel access data |
| Access to our environment | High | Read-only IAM role in every AWS account (SecurityAudit plus a custom policy), IdP admin-level read, HRIS read |
| Business criticality | Medium | If Vanta is down, the audit is delayed; production is not affected |
| Substitutability | Medium | Competitors exist (Drata, Secureframe); migrating evidence history is painful but bounded |
| Regulatory exposure | Medium | Employee personal data under GDPR/PIPEDA; the tool itself is not in our SOC 2 system boundary but its output is our evidence |

**Inherent risk: Tier 1 (critical).** Full review, SOC 2 report read, annual re-review.

## 4. Residual risk and recommendation

Vanta's technical controls are strong and unusually well documented for a vendor of its size: field-level encryption with HSM-backed keys, phishing-resistant internal authentication, an EU instance, a 72-hour breach clause and a 10-day subprocessor notice. Against that sits a 2025 incident that is precisely the failure mode this vendor must not have, and a thin SLA.

**Residual risk: Medium.** **Recommendation: Approve with conditions.**

1. Obtain the SOC 2 Type II report from the trust center; confirm the categories in scope, read the exceptions, and specifically look for change-management findings covering the May 2025 period (F-01, F-06).
2. Ask the vendor directly, in writing: what changed in release management after the 2025 exposure; whether a customer-facing audit log of evidence access exists; whether MFA can be enforced for all our users (F-01, F-04).
3. Review the VantaAdditionalPermissions policy before connecting each AWS account and re-review it whenever Vanta publishes a policy update; keep the diff in the vendor file (F-03).
4. Choose the EU or US instance deliberately and record the choice; subscribe to the subprocessor list (F-06).
5. At contract: request a 30-day deletion commitment at termination and confirmation of deletion in writing (F-05); note the 99% SLA in the audit-planning risk register so evidence exports are taken before the audit window rather than relied on live (F-02).
6. Treat Vanta's own trust page as the model for ours, and treat the tool as one source of evidence, not the system of record: export evidence to our own storage quarterly.
